What this app is

It rebuilds the mathlets.org “Isoclines” tool and adds a guided explanation around it. The left window shows the plane −4 ≤ x ≤ 4, −4 ≤ y ≤ 4. The right column has four tabs: Basics (what the words mean and how each object is drawn), Every equation (the complete mathlet with notes on all eight dropdown equations), Problem 1 (y′ = y(1 − y) and the three initial conditions) and Problem 2 (y′ = y/x and the point (1, 1)). Each tab is a short sequence of steps; every step has a blue “do this” box, its own controls, and a live explanation in the floating + explanation panel that recomputes whenever you move a slider or click the graph.

Glossary

Differential equation (DE) of the form y′ = f(x, y)
A rule that hands you a slope. Put in a point (x, y), get out the number f(x, y), and that number is the slope dy/dx any solution passing through that point must have there. Example: for y′ = y(1 − y) the point (3, 2) gets slope 2(1 − 2) = −2.
Slope field (direction field)
Do that at every point of a grid and draw a short dash tilted at the computed slope. Nothing is solved; it is just the rule f evaluated many times and drawn. Long before you solve anything the field already shows where curves must rise, fall or level off.
Isocline
“Iso” = same, “cline” = slope. The isocline for the value m is the set of all points where the equation gives slope exactly m, i.e. the curve f(x, y) = m. Along it every dash has the same tilt m. In the mathlet you draw one by moving the m slider; here you can also press a button. The isocline itself is usually not a solution: it only organises the dashes.
Nullcline / zero isocline
The isocline for m = 0: the places where the dashes are flat. Solution curves crossing it have a horizontal tangent there (a local max or min), so it is the first thing to locate.
Equilibrium (constant) solution
A horizontal line y = c that is itself a solution. That happens exactly when f(x, c) = 0 for all x, e.g. y = 0 and y = 1 for y′ = y(1 − y). Other solutions cannot cross an equilibrium line.
Solution curve (integral curve)
The graph of a function y(x) that obeys the rule everywhere: at each of its points its steepness equals f(x, y), so the slope changes from point to point along the curve. “Tangent to the dash” means: zoom in on the curve at any point until it looks straight; that straight direction has exactly the slope of the dash the equation gives there. It is drawn by starting at a chosen point and repeatedly stepping a little in the direction the dash points (Euler’s method); the app uses a finer version of that (Runge–Kutta 4) so the curve is smooth.
Initial condition
The one point you insist the curve must pass through, written y(x₀) = y₀. In the mathlet you supply it by clicking on the graph. Choosing it picks one curve out of the whole family.
“As x tends to infinity”
Follow the curve to the right, further and further. Does its height settle at a number, or run off to +∞ or −∞ (possibly by shooting off at a vertical asymptote)?
Existence and uniqueness
If f and ∂f/∂y are continuous near a point, exactly one solution passes through it, so solution curves never cross there. Where f is undefined (e.g. x = 0 for y′ = y/x) that guarantee is lost: at the origin infinitely many solutions meet.

Removing things

Every button that adds curves or isoclines is a toggle: press it again to remove what it added (it shows blue while its curves are on screen). Clicking on the graph near an existing solution curve or isocline deletes that one; clicking empty space adds. Clear All / Clear Solutions / Clear Isoclines wipe everything of that kind.

Controls

Move the pointer over the graph to read x, y and the slope y′ at that point. Click (or tap) to select a point; in steps about solution curves a click also draws the solution through it, exactly like the mathlet. Slope Field toggles the dashes. Clear All / Clear Solutions / Clear Isoclines behave as in the mathlet. Picture mode switches to the dark look used in the course’s solution pictures. The explanation panel can be dragged by its bar, resized from its lower-right corner, minimised, closed, or sent to its own tab (which closes the floating panel; the tab keeps updating live).
