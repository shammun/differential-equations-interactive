What this app is

A complete walk-through of Recitation 13. The left window is a live picture; the right column is a sequence of tabs, each split into small steps. Every step has a blue Do this box telling you exactly which control to touch, and the floating + explanation panel recomputes every number for the settings you have chosen. Tabs 1–2 build the vocabulary (slope field, isocline, solution curve, critical point, phase line, stability, bifurcation) because the recitation assumes all of it; tabs 3–8 are parts (a) to (f) and the final recommendation.

Glossary — every term used in the solutions

Autonomous equation
An equation ẏ = f(y) whose right-hand side contains no t. The rule depends on where you are, never on when. Consequence: the slope field is made of horizontal stripes, and shifting a solution sideways in time gives another solution.
ẏ (y-dot)
dy/dt — the rate of change of the population in kilocrabs per month. The dot always means "per unit time".
f(y)
The right-hand side, here −¼ + y − y²/a. It is a formula whose input is y and whose output is the velocity. Its graph (a downward parabola) is the single most useful picture in the problem.
f′(y)
df/dy = 1 − 2y/a: the slope of that parabola, differentiated with respect to y, not t. It is not ÿ. (In fact ÿ = f′(y)·f(y), which is zero at every equilibrium and therefore useless for classifying them.)
Slope field / direction field
At each point of a grid in the (t, y) plane, draw a short dash whose slope is the number the equation returns there. Nothing is solved; the rule is simply evaluated many times.
Isocline
"Same slope": the set of points where the equation returns one fixed value m, i.e. f(y) = m. For an autonomous equation this is one or more horizontal lines, because the condition involves only y.
Nullcline (m = 0 isocline)
Where the dashes are flat. For an autonomous equation these horizontal lines are themselves solutions — the equilibria.
Critical point / equilibrium / constant solution
A number y* with f(y*) = 0. The constant function y(t) ≡ y* solves the equation. On the graph of f it is a place where the parabola meets the horizontal axis.
Solution curve / integral curve
The graph of a function y(t) obeying the rule at every one of its points. Built by starting at an initial condition and repeatedly stepping in the direction the dash points (Euler's method; the app uses the finer Runge–Kutta 4).
Phase line
The y-axis alone, carrying a dot at each critical point and an arrow in each gap showing which way solutions travel there (up if f > 0, down if f < 0). It is the graph of f with all the detail thrown away except the signs.
Stable / unstable / semi-stable
Stable: arrows on both sides point in (f′ < 0), and nearby herds return. Unstable: both arrows point out (f′ > 0). Semi-stable: the parabola touches the axis without crossing, so one arrow points in and the other points away (f = f′ = 0). Mathlet colours: green, red, cyan.
Discriminant
The b² − 4ac under the square root of the quadratic formula. Here it is a² − a; its sign decides whether there are two equilibria, one, or none.
Bifurcation diagram
All the phase lines at once: horizontal axis a, vertical axis y, and above each a you see that farm's phase line. The curve drawn is the locus of critical points.
Saddle-node (fold) bifurcation
The event at a = 1 where two equilibria collide and annihilate. It happens exactly where the graph of f becomes tangent to the axis, i.e. where f = 0 and f′ = 0 hold together.
Kilocrab
1000 crabs. The harvest of 250 crabs/month is 0.25 kilocrab/month — that is the −¼ in the equation.

Controls

Move the pointer over the picture for a live readout. Clicking does something different in each scene: on the parabola it moves the probe, on the (t, y) plane it starts a solution curve there, on the bifurcation diagram it sets a. Buttons under the picture are toggles. The explanation panel can be dragged by its bar, resized from its corner, minimised, closed, or sent to its own tab (which closes the floating panel and keeps updating live).
