6. The physical picture

ẋ + kx = k cos(ωt)

The ocean rises and falls with the tide: its level is the input qe(t) = cos ωt. Water flows through a channel into a bay, whose level x(t) is the response. The rate of change of the bay level is proportional to the difference between the two levels: ẋ = k(cos ωt − x). A wide, dredged channel (large k) lets the bay react quickly; a silted one (small k) makes it sluggish.

8. Why A = cos φ (the triangle in section 3)

Replace the input by the complex exponential eiωt and try z = H eiωt. Substituting gives H(k + iω) = k, so

H = k / (k + iω)

The complex number k + iω is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs k (along the real axis) and ω (up the imaginary axis). Dividing by it divides the length and subtracts the angle:

A = |H| = k / √(k² + ω²) = cos φ,  φ = arctan(ω/k)

So the amplitude is literally the cosine of the phase lag: they always move in opposite directions. Make the ω leg taller (faster tide) and the angle opens while k/hyp shrinks; make the k leg longer (wider channel) and the angle closes while k/hyp approaches 1.

10. The two cases from the problem

Case (i): k increases, ω fixed

Physical story. The channel is dredged. Water passes more freely, so the bay reacts faster (reaction time ≈ 1/k drops) and can follow the ocean more closely.

Amplitude. ω/k gets smaller, so A = 1/√(1 + (ω/k)²) rises toward 1. Limit k → ∞: the bay is the ocean, A = 1. Limit k → 0: plugged channel, A = 0.

Phase lag. φ = arctan(ω/k) falls toward 0: a quick bay is hardly late. Answer: A increases, φ decreases.

Case (ii): ω increases, k fixed

Physical story. The Earth spins faster, so tides come more often. The channel is unchanged, so the bay's reaction time ≈ 1/k is the same as before.

Amplitude. ω/k grows, so A falls toward 0: the ocean flips before the bay can fill, and the ups and downs cancel. Limit ω → 0: infinitely slow tide, the bay keeps up, A = 1.

Phase lag. The same reaction time is now a bigger fraction of a shorter cycle, so φ rises toward π/2 (a quarter cycle behind). Answer: A decreases, φ increases.

Both cases are the same fact seen from two sides: everything depends on the ratio ω/k. Raising k lowers the ratio; raising ω raises it. Changing both at once? Only their ratio matters for A and φ (the time lag t₀ = φ/ω does also depend on ω itself).
