AMPLITUDE AND PHASE: SECOND ORDER II
+ explanation
+ help

At left is a spring–mass–dashpot system. The spring hangs from a fixed support and carries the mass; the mass's rod is the piston of a dashpot whose cylinder is moved up and down by a driving piston at the bottom.

The equation governing the system is shown in orange at the top. The mass is 1; b is the damping constant, k the spring constant, and ω the circular frequency of the sinusoidal motion of the driving piston.

In the graphing window the position of the top of the dashpot — the input signal cos(ωt) — is drawn in blue, and the position of the mass (the system response x) in orange. Diamonds mark the current values of cos(ωt) and x; the gray vertical line between them is the extension of the spring. The time lag t0 is read out in dark blue at the bottom of the screen, below the period P. When t0 > 0 it is also shown in the graph as a gray vertical line measured by a dark blue segment.

Move the cursor over the graphing window to get crosshairs and a readout of t and x. Holding the mouse button down hides the crosshairs. The horizontal crosshair is continued into the window showing the system.

Use the slider handle below the graph to choose a value of t. The [>>] key animates the system; while running it reads [||] and stops the animation when pressed. At the end of the run it reads [<<], which resets t to zero.

Drag the [b], [k] or [ω] slider to change those parameters.

The [Bode plots] key shows or hides two windows on the right. The upper one graphs the amplitude A of the sinusoidal response against ω; the lower one graphs the negative of the phase lag, −φ, against ω.

The [Nyquist plot] key shows or hides a window at lower right containing part of the complex plane. The gray curve is the path of the complex gain biω / p(iω), where p(s) = s2 + bs + k is the characteristic polynomial, as ω runs over positive values. The orange diamond is the gain at the chosen ω, joined to the origin by an orange segment whose length is the amplitude A; the angle from the positive real axis, marked by the green arc, is −φ.

Move the cursor over the amplitude window to draw an orange horizontal line there and in the graphing window marking the maximal displacement, with a readout of that maximum. Move the cursor over the phase window for a readout of the phase shift.

Note: these are not true Bode or Nyquist plots. A Bode plot uses log(A) against log(ω) and −φ against log(ω); a Nyquist plot lets ω run from −∞ to +∞, adding a mirror-image portion above the real axis.
