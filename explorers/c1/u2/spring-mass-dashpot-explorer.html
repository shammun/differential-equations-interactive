Why a complex root means the cart oscillates

Every solution is built from ert. So the whole question is: what does ert look like when r is real, and what does it look like when r has an imaginary part? Write the root as

r = α + iω     α = real part (decay or growth rate)     ω = imaginary part (turning rate)
Set α and ω by hand here — independent of m, b, k — so you can isolate what each one does.

Step 2 — why a real root can cross zero at most once

With two real roots the solution is c₁er₁t + c₂er₂t. Setting it to zero and dividing gives e(r₁−r₂)t = −c₂/c₁. The left side is a strictly monotone curve, so it can hit the flat target line at most one time — no matter what the constants are. Drag the target line to test it.
